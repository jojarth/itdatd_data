# Forms and Species

Every creature in this book carries three kinds of keywords that describe what it is. Its **Essence** tells you what sustains it, which is covered in the previous chapter. Its **Form** tells you the shape of its body. Its **Species** tells you what kind of creature it is.

These three vary independently. An owlbear and a dragon share the same Form, even though one is an ordinary living animal and the other is a legend given flesh.

<div class="green-table">

| **Category** | **The Question** | **How Many** |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Essence** | What sustains it? | Exactly one |
| **Form** | What shape is its body? | One or more |
| **Species** | What kind of creature is it? | One or more |

</div>

## Forms

Form describes the shape of a creature's body and how that body interacts with the world. It says nothing about where the creature came from or what keeps it alive.

A creature has at least one Form keyword. Creatures whose bodies combine more than one shape have more than one; a centaur is both **Humanoid** and **Beast**.

<div class="green-table">

| **Form** | **Description** | **Examples** |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Humanoid** | An upright body with two arms, two legs, and hands that can use tools and gear | Humans, dwarves, goblins, giants, imps |
| **Beast** | An animal body that could exist in nature | Wolves, horses, snakes, giant spiders |
| **Monstrosity** | A hybrid, impossible, or unnatural body | Owlbears, griffons, dragons, hydras |
| **Plant** | A vegetative or fungal body | Treants, shambling mounds, killer vines |
| **Amorphous** | A body with no fixed shape, made of flowing substance | Oozes, living flame, walking smoke |
| **Swarm** | Many tiny bodies acting as a single creature | Rat swarms, bat clouds, swarms of bees |
| **Incorporeal** | No physical body at all | Ghosts, shades, wraiths |
| **Object** | An inanimate thing given motion | Animated swords, rugs, and statues |

</div>

### Assigning Forms
- **Size does not change Form.** A giant spider is still a **Beast**, because its body is a spider's body, only larger.
- **Wings and tails do not rule out Humanoid.** If a creature stands upright and uses its hands as a person would, it is **Humanoid**.
- **Tiny creatures usually appear as Swarms.** A single bee or fly rarely matters in play, so its entry is the swarm.
- **Beast or Monstrosity?** Ask whether the body could exist in nature. A wolf could; a creature with the head of an owl and the body of a bear could not.

## Species

Every creature has at least one Species keyword. Most have two: a **group** it shares with similar creatures, and its own **kind**.

- **Group** keywords gather related creatures that rules often treat as one, such as **Insect**, **Equine**, **Canine**, **Vermin**, or **Devil**. A creature may belong to more than one group; a fly is both **Insect** and **Vermin**.
- **Kind** keywords name the creature itself, using the name a common person would give it, such as **Bee**, **Horse**, **Wolf**, or **Imp**.

When a creature's group and kind are the same word, such as **Dragon**, **Dwarf**, or **Human**, it is listed only once. A creature whose heritage spans more than one people possesses the Species keywords of each.

### Assigning Species
- **Size, age, and color do not change kind.** A giant bee is still a **Bee**, a dire wolf is still a **Wolf**, and a red dragon is still a **Dragon**.
- **Training does not change kind.** A war horse is a **Horse**. If a rule needs to tell trained creatures apart, that is an Adventure keyword, such as **Warhorse**.
