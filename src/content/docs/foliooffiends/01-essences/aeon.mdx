Ask a villager about the dragon in the mountains and they'll tell you it's a god, or a punishment, or the reason the harvest failed. They'll tell you their grandmother saw it once and never spoke above a whisper again. Most of them have never seen it, and they would like to keep it that way.

They are not entirely wrong. Aeon creatures are legends given flesh. A dragon is not simply a very large lizard that breathes fire; it is the idea of the dragon, ancient and hungry and proud, made real. Aeon creatures embody something older and larger than mortal life, and they do not age or die the way mortals do. Some are born, some simply *are*, and a few may be older than the world's oldest stories.

## Telling Them Apart

### Aeon or Animus?
This is the line you will draw most often. An owlbear is a magical beast, but it was born, it will grow old, and it will die. It is Animus. A dragon may also have been hatched, but it does not grow old in any way that matters, and its existence carries a weight that ordinary life doesn't. It is Aeon.

If a creature is remarkable but you could imagine a hunter skinning it and selling the hide, it is probably Animus. If the hunter would be cursed for the rest of his life just for trying, it might be Aeon.

### Aeon or Fey?
Both can be ancient, and both can be tied to a place. A Fey guardian is bound by its custom: it guards the bridge because that is its oath, and it cannot do otherwise. An Aeon guardian guards the mountain because it *is* the mountain's might made flesh. Fey creatures are defined by their rules. Aeon creatures are defined by what they embody.

### Aeon or Cosmic?
Aeon creatures belong to the world, however vast they are. They are part of its oldest order. Cosmic creatures do not belong at all.

## Example Creatures
- **Dragons**, ancient and hoarding, worshipped and feared by everyone who lives within a week's ride of their lair.
- **Phoenixes**, which burn and are reborn, and whose ashes are worth a kingdom to the right buyer.
- **The mountain-guardian**, a stone-skinned giant that has slept beneath the same peak since before the first dwarf hold was dug.
- **Celestial beasts**, such as a great white stag said to appear only before the death of a king.

## Running Aeon Creatures
Aeon creatures should be rare. Most adventurers never meet one, and those who do should remember it for the rest of their lives, however short those may be. If the party meets an Aeon creature every other session, they stop being legends.

Give them presence before you give them stat blocks. Let the party find the scorched valley, the bones of the last expedition, and the village that leaves out tribute every new moon long before they see the creature itself. By the time it arrives, the players should already be afraid of it.

Aeon creatures are also rarely mindless. Many are old enough to have opinions about kings that died centuries ago. A party that cannot win a fight against one can sometimes win a conversation, a bargain, or a very fast retreat.

:::tip[Not everything big is Aeon]{icon="information"}
Giant spiders, cave bears, and the great wyrm-eels of the deep rivers are all dangerous, and all of them are Animus. Save Aeon for creatures that people tell stories about, not just creatures that are large.
:::
