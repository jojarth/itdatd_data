Most folk in Cerrix never think about Animus at all, the same way they never think about the air. It's simply what life is: you're born, you grow, you work, you age, and one day you die. Everything else is strange by comparison.

That familiarity is deceptive. Animus creatures include the peasant tilling the field, but also the wolf pack that takes his sheep, the bandit who takes his coin, and the owlbear that takes the bandit. Being ordinary has never made anything harmless. Most of the creatures that kill adventurers are Animus, and most of the adventurers they kill are, too.

## Telling Them Apart

### Animus or Aeon?
See [Aeon](/foliooffiends/01-essences/aeon/). In short: if people tell stories about the creature itself, it might be Aeon. If they only tell stories about the time it ate someone's cousin, it is Animus.

### Animus or Elemental?
A creature that breathes fire, lives in a volcano, or swims through freezing water is still Animus if it was born and needs to eat. An Elemental's body *is* the fire or the water.

### Staying Animus
A creature does not stop being Animus because it was blessed, cursed, transformed, or altered. A knight turned into a toad is an Animus toad. A man possessed by a demon is still an Animus man, with an Umbra creature inside him. Its Essence changes only when the thing keeping it alive changes, such as when a vampire's victim rises from the grave.

## Example Creatures
- **Humans, dwarves, gnomes, halflings, and half-orcs**, along with most other mortal peoples.
- **Beasts** of every kind, from wolves and boars to cave bears and the great wyrm-eels of deep rivers.
- **Magical beasts** such as owlbears, griffons, and basilisks. Their anatomy may be strange and their abilities supernatural, but they are born, and they die.
- **Monstrous peoples** such as goblins and ogres.
- **Strange plants**, such as a thornwood that strangles travelers, if it grew from a seed.

## Running Animus Creatures
Animus creatures behave like living things. They are hungry, scared, territorial, and tired. A wolf pack that has lost two members will usually run. A goblin war band that has lost its chief will probably start arguing about who's next. Use morale, and remember that most living creatures would rather survive than win.

This is also what makes them useful. Animus creatures can be bargained with, bribed, frightened off, tracked, trapped, and starved out in ways that a golem or a ghost cannot. Reward players who think of them as animals and people rather than as hit point totals.
