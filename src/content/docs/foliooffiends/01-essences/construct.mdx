To most people, a Construct is a tool: a very large, very dangerous tool that some wizard built and then probably lost control of. The bronze guardian outside the old temple has been killing grave robbers for three hundred years, and nobody alive remembers how to tell it to stop.

That's usually true, but not always. A Construct is any creature that was *made* rather than born. Its body was shaped by hands, and its motion comes from enchantment, a bound spirit, or stranger engineering. Some Constructs think, and a few feel. Intelligence doesn't change what a Construct is. The question is never whether it can think, but whether someone built it.

## Telling Them Apart

### Construct or Necros?
This is the line most GMs will stumble over. If the dead are walking because death itself is keeping them up, as with zombies, skeletons, wights, and ghouls, they are Necros. If someone treated corpses as *material*, carving and stitching and binding them into something new, the result is a Construct. A shambling skeleton raised from its grave is Necros. A towering golem of fused bones built in a sorcerer's workshop is a Construct.

### Construct or Elemental?
A clay golem is a Construct: someone shaped the clay and bound a spirit to animate it. A living mudslide that rose on its own from a flooded riverbank is Elemental. If no one built it, it isn't a Construct.

## Example Creatures
- **Golems** of clay, stone, iron, or flesh.
- **Bronze temple guardians**, still following orders their makers gave centuries ago.
- **A scarecrow** animated by a hedge-witch to keep more than crows out of her fields.
- **Clockwork servants** from a long-dead artificer's tower, polishing silver for masters who have been dust for a hundred years.
- **Bone-golems**, built from the remains of the dead but animated by enchantment, not by death.

## Running Construct Creatures
Constructs follow instructions, often too literally and for far too long. Ask what a Construct was built to do, and then play it exactly that way. A guardian told to "let no one pass" may ignore a thief who climbs over the wall, because climbing over isn't passing. A servant told to "keep the house clean" may treat muddy adventurers as dirt.

Constructs don't usually check morale, they don't sleep, and they can't be bribed or frightened. That makes them dangerous in a straight fight and very satisfying to outwit. Give players a chance to discover the instructions, the command word, or the maker's mark, and let them turn the Construct's purpose against it.

:::tip[Intelligent Constructs]{icon="information"}
Some Constructs think, feel, and want. They may resent their purpose, mourn their makers, or have long since decided to ignore both. They are still Constructs, and healing a damaged one still takes a craftsman's tools rather than a priest's prayers.
:::
