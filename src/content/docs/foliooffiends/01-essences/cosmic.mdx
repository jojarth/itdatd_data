The miners who broke into the old shaft came back talking about a light that had no color and a sound that came from inside their teeth. Two of them never came back at all. The village priest called it a demon. The village priest was wrong.

Cosmic creatures come from outside the world's rules. Some fell from beyond the stars, some crawled up from places that cannot exist, and some may have always been here, hiding in the corners of reality where mortals don't look. Their bodies, thoughts, and motives don't follow natural law. Some bend space, memory, or time just by being near. They are alien, and that alone makes them terrifying.

Here is what the priest got wrong: a Cosmic creature is not opposed to life. It is *alive*, in its own impossible way, and it may not be hostile at all. It is simply foreign to everything mortals understand.

## Cosmic Gameplay
All Cosmic creatures share the following traits.

  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="The Question">

  </FeatureSection>

  Cosmic creatures are not automatically **Defiled**. Their strangeness can be deadly without being corrupt. A Cosmic creature that has become Defiled is something truly dreadful.
  </FeatureSection>

## Telling Them Apart

### Cosmic or Umbra?
Both are frightening, and mortals mix them up constantly. The difference is in how each relates to the world. An Umbra creature *works against* the world's order: it corrupts, tempts, and degrades, and its motives are often all too easy to understand. A Cosmic creature *doesn't fit* the world's order at all. It may want something no mortal can comprehend, or nothing that has anything to do with mortals. A demon wants your soul. A Cosmic creature may not know you have one.

### Cosmic or Aeon?
Aeon creatures are part of the world's oldest order. Cosmic creatures were never part of it.

## Example Creatures
- **The thing in the mine**, a pulsing mass of colorless light that fell from the sky long ago and has been dreaming ever since.
- **A mind that speaks through dreams**, whispering mathematics to sleeping scholars until they wake and begin to build something.
- **Hounds from the angles**, lean things that move between moments and are only ever seen in the corner of the eye.
- **A visitor** that wears a merchant's shape almost perfectly, except that it never blinks and its shadow falls the wrong way.

## Running Cosmic Creatures
Cosmic creatures work best when they are used sparingly and explained very little. Describe what the characters perceive, not what the creature is. The light has no color. The room is larger on the inside. Everyone remembers the stranger's name differently. Let the players be uneasy.

Don't make every Cosmic creature hostile. Some are curious, some are indifferent, and a few are even kind in ways that are just as frightening as cruelty. The horror comes from not understanding what it wants, not from knowing it wants to kill you.

:::tip[It still bleeds]{icon="information"}
Because Cosmic creatures are Living, the party's ordinary tools still work on them, at least a little. That's important. Cosmic horror in ITD&TD is about the unknown, not about hopelessness. A clever, brave, or desperate party should always have a chance.
:::
