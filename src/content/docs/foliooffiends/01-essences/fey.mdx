Every child in Cerrix knows the rules. Don't eat food offered under the hill. Don't give your true name to a stranger on the old road. Always say thank you to the house for keeping you warm, and never, ever step inside a ring of mushrooms. The children who forget the rules are the ones in the stories.

Fey creatures are bound by custom, place, and oath. Their existence is tied to something meaningful: an ancient forest, a hidden road, a name, a promise, a festival, a river, or a ruin. They follow strange rules as strictly as mortals follow gravity. A Fey creature that swore to guard a bridge cannot leave it, and one that was invited in may not need to leave at all. They can be kind, cruel, generous, and deadly all at once, especially when nobody understands their rules.

## Telling Them Apart

### Fey or Animus?
Elves are the most common Fey creatures most people will ever meet, and they are easy to mistake for Animus. They are born, they grow, and they eventually die, much like humans. But elves are sustained by old custom and place in a way humans are not, which is why their bodies resist things that would harm a mortal. Half-elves are Animus, with a touch of Fey in their blood.

### Fey or Elemental?
See [Elemental](/foliooffiends/01-essences/elemental/). In short: Fey creatures care about promises. Elementals usually don't understand them.

### Fey or Necros?
A ghost bound to the place where it died can look a lot like a Fey guardian bound to a grave. Ask what keeps it going. If it is kept going by death, it's Necros. If it's kept going by an oath, a custom, or the place itself, it's Fey.

## Example Creatures
- **Elves**, the Fey folk mortals know best.
- **A will-o'-the-wisp** bound to a marsh, leading travelers into the deep water because that is what will-o'-the-wisps do.
- **A hearth-spirit** that keeps a farmhouse warm and lucky, and sours the milk for a year if anyone forgets to thank it.
- **The bridge-keeper**, who asks every traveler three questions and cannot harm anyone who answers truthfully.
- **The lords and ladies of a hidden court**, beneath a hill where time runs strangely and every gift has a price.
- **Nymphs, satyrs, and sprites**, tied to their springs, groves, and glades.

## Running Fey Creatures
Every Fey creature should have at least one rule it must follow. Write it down before play, and make sure the players can discover it, through lore, observation, or a story an innkeeper tells. The rule is how a weaker party survives a stronger Fey creature. Fey creatures are often dangerous in a fair fight and helpless against someone who knows their rules.

Fey creatures also love bargains. Let the players make deals, and then hold everyone to the exact words. Fey creatures should keep their side of a bargain perfectly, and they should expect the same.

:::tip[Courtesy is a weapon]{icon="information"}
A party that walks into a Fey glade swinging swords should have a very bad day. A party that says please, accepts nothing for free, and leaves a gift at the threshold should find that many Fey creatures are surprisingly reasonable.
:::
