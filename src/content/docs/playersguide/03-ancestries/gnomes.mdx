Gnomes are a very strange race, one that shares many similarities to both dwarves and elves. In fact, some scholars state, but cannot prove, that gnomes are descended from dwarves and gnomes copulating.  They tend to value the same scholarly pursuits as elves, but with a much more serious and dour outlook. 

Gnomes are short, rarely reaching 4 feet tall. Their culture promotes the needs of the self, rather than that of the community, so many gnomes end up being very selfish and vain, often doing what they need to do to get themselves ahead at the cost of others. This selfishness doesn't extend to their friends, as they are a very loyal to those who show them loyalty, which may be their saving grace. Thus, a gnomish employer would actually treat their loyal employees very well, right down to the smallest jobs. However, they would treat the workers of for a different employer with a large amount of disdain. They aren't a cruel people, but they aren't kind either.  This outlook has caused most races to only reach out to gnomes rarely, which is just fine by gnomes. However, once you prove yourself to a gnome, you've made a friend that would take a crossbow bolt for you. 

Gnome civilizations tend to value the freedom of the individual, and any attempt to manipulate these freedoms is seen as taboo. Because of this, gnome societies tend to breed more criminal activity than dwarven or elven cultures, but not as much as human ones either. Law abiding gnomes tend to get upset at the inconvenience a criminal causes, rather than the actual criminal acts they perform. Gnomes only deal with other races out of necessity in most cases, however some gnomes have a good thing going with other races, and see the value in cooperation, if only for their own gain. Gnomes aren't necessarily evil, nor is gnomish culture, but it's easy to see why people would think it from the outside looking in. 

Like elves, much of what is known about how the world works is due to a gnomish scholars, whose incredible focus allows them to concentrate on tasks that others simply cannot. Although gnomes don't make great craftsmen, they make great scholars, and business owners, and ruthless entrepreneurs.   

Gnomes men and women are vain, valuing physical appearance much more than most. Their faces are rarely blemished, their hair always combed. Gnome men grow facial hair, with fantastic moustaches, but less than stellar beards, and like their dwarven brethren, they keep their facial hair well-kempt. Since their beards tend to be (although not always) patchy, many male gnomes keep their face clean shaven, but take great pains to keep their moustaches up to date with the latest styles. Gnome women like to “show off the goods” as it's known, dressing in modest, but form-fitting dress. All gnomes tend to dress lavishly, and even poor gnomes will spend much of their silver on clothing that doesn't befit their lifestyle.

Since gnome culture values the individual so much, they tend to be self-sufficient, intelligent and hearty. Gnome adventurers tend to be scholarly, or brought along for their various known skills, often for more than a fair share of the treasure

    </FeatureSection>

## Gnome Ages
Gnomes age lightly, their quickness of hand and mind carrying them well into later years. As time passes, their curiosity sharpens into eccentric brilliance, though their bodies begin to lag behind their thoughts. In old age, they become strange and insightful in equal measure, never quite losing their spark.

To randomly roll your starting age, you must first determine your class keywords. If you have 1 class keyword, take the Base Age for the keyword, and add the result of the Roll for the keyword as well. If you have more than 1 class keyword, you use the highest base age for your keywords, but roll all dice in the roll column for each class keyword you have.

Shareece is 76 years old, starting her adventuring career at a mature age.

After determining your age, you apply all age factors below for your current age, and every age lower. Thus, an elf who starts in their prime must step through all Young and Prime attribute adjustments. Note that some age requirements are optional. 

As a reminder, during character creation, your attributes cannot go above 19, nor below 6 due to aging bonuses and penalties. Simply ignore any bonuses or penalties that would adjust your attributes so. 

Some age breakpoints overlap; and when aging a character, once you hit the age breakpoint, you can choose to delay your attribute adjustments for up to 1 year, but must apply the attribute adjustments before you reach the age that doesn't overlap. For example, 120 is both Young and Prime. Once you reach 120, you can choose to apply the attribute adjustments for being Prime, but you do not have to. During any rest between age 120 and 121 you can decide to apply the attribute adjustments, but it must be done by the time you turn 121, or it is done the first rest you have once you turn 121. 

### Young Gnomes (20 - 40)
Young gnomes are viewed as disruptive apprentices; tolerated for potential, but closely supervised due to curiosity and unpredictability.

Young gnomes are allowed to increase either their Agility, Precision, or Logic attributes by 1, but if they do so, they must reduce either their Brawn, Heartiness, or Reason by 1. 

### Prime Gnomes (40 - 75)
Gnomes in their prime are valued creators, inventors, and problem-solvers; expected to produce results, even when their methods are unconventional.

Gnomes in their prime must select two attributes from Agility, Precision, Logic, and Reason, and increase the selected attributes by 1 point each. 

### Mature Gnomes (75 - 125)
Mature gnomes are respected eccentrics and specialists; their insight is sought, though their reasoning is not always followed.

Gnomes who reach a mature age must increase their Precision, Logic, and Savvy by 1 point each. However, their Brawn is reduced by 1 point as well.

### Aging Gnomes (125 - 175)
Aging gnomes are seen as increasingly strange but useful; communities consult them carefully, often through intermediaries.

Gnomes who reach this age will increase their Precision, Logic, Reason, and Savvy by 1 point each. However, their Brawn, Heartiness, and Presence are reduced by 1 points each. 

### Elderly Elves (175+)
Upon reaching the Elderly age, roll 10d10 and note the result. Add this result to 175, and you find the age you will die of old age. Elderly gnomes are treated as near-mythic minds; either revered as geniuses or quietly avoided depending on temperament and reputation.

Gnomes who reach this age will increase their Logic, Reason, Savvy, and Presence by 1 point each. However, their Brawn, Heartiness, Agility, and Precision are reduced by 1 points each.
