Unlike all the other races, humans are a vast and differing race. Some humans have made their own culture, some humans have adopted parts of other cultures, or even a mish-mash of cultures. Humans are so varied, that they actually intimidate other races. Whereas you generally know what an elven society will be like when you enter it, you never know what you’re going to get with humans. 

Humans tend to stand as the tallest of the player races, although with how varied they are, this isn’t always true. They also run the gamut of weights, from incredibly skinny to incredibly fat, and all the weights in-between. Humans are so varied that it’s hard to pin them down, thus humans don’t really have outlying ability scores like other races do.

Like the people in them, human civilizations are a varied place. Many towns have a “good section” and “bad section” of towns. The wealthy can either be altruistic, using their wealth to help others, or terrible slave drivers, earning their wealth because of their ruthlessness. The poor could be poor by choice, giving everything to charity, or poor because of oppression, and not knowing how to fight it. Variety is the name of the game with Humans.
   
Humans reach adulthood around 15 years old, although they don’t tend to adventure until they are in their late teens. Humans are considered to be middle age at 35, old age at 55, and venerable at 70. Like other things in life, humans have a variable range of ages that they live until, but rarely living beyond 80.
   
Humans are the tallest of the player-available races, they tend to be between 5 feet and 6. Although some females have been known to be shorter than 5 feet, and some males as large as 7. 
    
Since human culture varies so much, it’s difficult to pin-down human adventures. They run the gamut from wizened scholars to mighty warriors, and everything in between.

## Human Ages
Humans come into strength quickly and spend it just as fast, their best years brief but bright. They grow wiser as they weaken, trading vigor for judgment in a steady, inevitable decline. Few escape the weight of age, and fewer still remain formidable in both body and mind for long.

To randomly roll your starting age, you must first determine your class keywords. If you have 1 class keyword, take the Base Age for the keyword, and add the result of the Roll for the keyword as well. If you have more than 1 class keyword, you use the highest base age for your keywords, but roll all dice in the roll column for each class keyword you have.

:::tip[Two Humans]{icon="information"}
William has selected the Thief class, which contains the Adept class keyword. Thus, he rolls 3d8, getting 12. He adds that to the base age of 12, and William is 24 years old, just about in his prime. 

However, Joan is a Cleric\Fighter which has the Priest and Soldier keywords. Since the base age of priest is higher, her base age is 18. She then rolls 3d8 for her priest, getting 16, and 2d6 for her Fighter, getting a 4. Joan is 38 (18 + 16 + 4), still in her prime, but getting close to mature. 
:::

After determining your age, you apply all age factors below for your current age, and every age lower. Thus, an elf who starts in their prime must step through all Young and Prime attribute adjustments. Note that some age requirements are optional. 

As a reminder, during character creation, your attributes cannot go above 19, nor below 6 due to aging bonuses and penalties. Simply ignore any bonuses or penalties that would adjust your attributes so. 

Some age breakpoints overlap; and when aging a character, once you hit the age breakpoint, you can choose to delay your attribute adjustments for up to 1 year, but must apply the attribute adjustments before you reach the age that doesn't overlap. For example, 120 is both Young and Prime. Once you reach 120, you can choose to apply the attribute adjustments for being Prime, but you do not have to. During any rest between age 120 and 121 you can decide to apply the attribute adjustments, but it must be done by the time you turn 121, or it is done the first rest you have once you turn 121. 

### Young Humans (15 - 25)
Humans younger than 15 do not adventure. Young humans are not quite fully grown, but since they live so short, they start to be seen as adults around this age. However, they are also expected to prove themselves quickly, or be dismissed just as quick. 

Young humans are allowed to increase any attribute by 1, but if they do so, they must reduce any other attribute by 1. 

### Prime Humans (25 - 40)
Humans in their prime are the backbone of society, making up the bulk of soldiers, laborers, and workers. 

Humans in their prime must select two different attributes increase the selected attributes by 1 point each. However, they must also select one attribute different from the other two, and reduce it by 1.

### Mature Humans (40 - 55)
Mature humans are  experienced and respected; often placed in charge or deferred to in disputes. They start to lose some of their physical prowess, but have gained in wisdom. 

Humans who reach a mature age increase their Logic, Reason, Savvy and Presence all by 1 point each. However, they must also select two attributes from the following list, and reduce the selected two by 1 point each: Brawn, Heartiness, Agility, or Precision. 

### Aging Humans (55- 70)
Aging humans are seen as slowing; valued for counsel, but rarely for labor or war. 

Aging Humans increase their Logic, Reason, Savvy and Presence all by 1 point each. However, they must also select two attributes from the following list, and reduce the selected two by 1 point each: Brawn, Heartiness, Agility, or Precision. 

### Elderly Humans (70+)
Upon reaching the Elderly age, roll 2d20 and note the result. Add this result to 70, and you find the age you will die of old age. Elderly humans can be seen as either revered elders or burdens, depending on their means and reputation.

Humans who reach this age will increase their Reason, Savvy, and Presence by 1 point each. However, their Brawn, Heartiness, Agility, and Precision are reduced by 1 points each. 

Humans have a special provision to them, as they age, they quickly lose some of what once made them so mighty. Upon reaching 80, and every 5 years thereafter (85, 90, 95, 100 etc...), Humans will reduce their Logic, Reason, Savvy, and Presence attribute by 1 point each, and their Brawn, Heartiness, Agility, and Presence by 2 points each. This trend continues until they die of old age, or one of their attributes reaches 0, and they die from that. 

## Height and Weight
To randomly determine your character's height and weight, start by rolling 2d10, record the total as “extra inches” and then roll a 2d4. Multiple your “extra inches” result by the result of the 2d4 roll to get your “extra weight”.

For males, add your “extra inches” to 58 inches to get how tall you are in inches, additionally, add the “extra weight” to 120 lbs. to get your starting weight. If you are female, add your “extra inches” to 53 inches, and add your “extra weight” to 85 lbs.
