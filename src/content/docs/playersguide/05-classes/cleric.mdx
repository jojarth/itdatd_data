## Cleric Features
As a cleric, you gain the following class features.

<GameplayCard
  color="purple"
  title="Holy Symbol"
  subeyebrow="1st Level Cleric Feature"
  headerOrder={['title', 'subeyebrow']}
  toc
>
Many of your class features require you to present your holy symbol. A holy symbol is a physical representation of your faith and devotion, and may take nearly any form, such as an amulet, prayer beads, a reliquary, or another relic sacred to your beliefs.

To present your holy symbol, you must hold it aloft in clear view. This requires a free hand. Alternatively, you may emblazon your holy symbol upon a shield. Presenting such a shield fulfills any requirement to present your holy symbol.
</GameplayCard>

<GameplayCard
  color="purple"
  title="Turn Undead"
  subeyebrow="1st Level Cleric Feature"
  headerOrder={['title', 'subeyebrow']}
  toc
>
Clerics can channel sacred power to drive back the undead. To use this ability, you must openly present a symbol of your faith. **Turn Undead** requires **1 Master Action** and may be performed beginning in **Phase 1**. You must present your holy symbol to turn undead. 

  <FeatureSection title="Determine Highest Level">
    When you attempt to Turn Undead, first determine the highest-level undead creature you can affect.

    Roll **1d20** and compare the result to your **Presence** Attribute using the table below. The result determines the highest creature level that can be affected by this attempt.

    Undead creatures **4 or more levels higher** than your Cleric Level cannot be affected. Likewise, undead creatures **3 or more levels lower** than your Cleric Level are always affected, regardless of your roll.

    <div class="yellow-table">
    |        **d20 Result**        | **Highest Creature Level** |
    | :-------------------------- | :------------------------: |
    | **Presence +6** | Cleric Level -2 |
    | **Presence +3** | Cleric Level -1 |
    | **Presence**    | Cleric Level    |
    | **Presence -3** | Cleric Level +1 |
    | **Presence -6** | Cleric Level +2 |
    | **Presence -9** | Cleric Level +3 |
    </div>
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Determine Turning Capacity">
    Next, determine the total number of creature levels you can affect. Roll **2d6** and add **half your Cleric Level** (rounded down). The result is your **Turning Capacity**.
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Turn Undead Effect">
    Beginning with the lowest-level Eligible Creature within **60 feet** that can see you, subtract each affected creature's level from your Turning Capacity until you either have insufficient capacity to affect another creature or there are no more eligible creatures.

    Affected creatures immediately flee from you by the safest available route. Instead of fleeing, creatures that are **5 or more levels lower** than your Cleric Level are instantly destroyed, crumbling to dust.
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Creature Interactions"> 
  * Creatures with the **Necros** keyword are Eligible Creatures. 
  </FeatureSection>
</GameplayCard>

<GameplayCard
  color="purple"
  title="Spellcasting"
  subeyebrow="2nd Level Cleric Feature"
  headerOrder={['title', 'subeyebrow']}
  toc
>
Through prayer and unwavering faith, you have learned to prepare sacred miracles within your mind. Each miracle is a sacred blessing that can be held only temporarily. When invoked, that blessing is expended and slips from memory until it is prepared again through prayer.

A Cleric's spells are considered clerical spells. 
  
  <FeatureSection title="Spell Preparation">
  Immediately after completing a Safe Rest, you may perform a personal ritual to prepare your spells. During this ritual, you offer prayers to a higher power, beseeching it to grant you sacred miracles. Each prepared spell occupies one spell slot of its level. Clerics may prepare any spell on the Cleric spell list, so long as they have the correct level of spell slot. 

  For example, a 4th-level Cleric prepares three 1st-level spells and two 2nd-level spells. Preparing the same spell multiple times allows you to cast it multiple times before resting again. Prepared spells remain in memory until cast.
  
  Spell Preparation can only be done immediately following a Safe Rest.

  </FeatureSection>
  
  <FeatureSection title="Casting Spells">
  To cast a prepared spell, you must:
  
  * Have the spell prepared.
  * Be able to present your holy symbol.
  * Be able to speak clearly.
 
  Some spells also require material components. If so, the required component is used in place of your holy symbol when casting the spell.
  
  After resolving the spell's effects, the sacred miracle fades from your memory. The spell slot becomes empty and cannot be used again until you prepare spells after completing another Safe Rest.
  </FeatureSection>
  
  <FeatureSection title="Cantrips">
  Cantrips are simple prayers that require little devotion to prepare.

  Whenever you prepare your spells after completing a Safe Rest, you also prepare a number of cantrips from the Cleric cantrip list. The Spell Progression table shows how many cantrips you may prepare, as well as the number of cantrip casts available in your shared pool.

  Each time you cast a prepared cantrip, you expend one available cantrip cast from your shared pool, regardless of which prepared cantrip you cast. Unlike leveled spells, cantrips are not erased from your memory when cast and remain prepared until you prepare your spells again.

  You regain all expended cantrip casts whenever you complete a night's rest.
  </FeatureSection>
</GameplayCard>
