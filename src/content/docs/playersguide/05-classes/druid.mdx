Druids are keepers of old mysteries: the hidden rhythms of the wild, the turning of fate, and the bonds between life, death, and memory. Their power grows through contemplation and personal journeys, each one revealing a new truth and a new way to shape the world around them.

## Druid Gameplay
Below is a list of information needed to play a Cleric at the various levels of play.

    <FeatureSection title="Class Requirements">
    To become a Druid, you must meet the following attribute requirements:
   
    - Your Logic score must be 12 or higher.
    - Your Reason score must be 14 or higher.
   
    </FeatureSection>
  <FeatureSection title="Class Keywords">
  **Priest**, **Druid**

  <FeatureSection title="Prime Requisite">
    Your prime requisites are logic and reason, and you gain bonus experience points based on your logic and reason scores.
    - If your logic and reason scores are 13 or higher, you gain a +5% bonus to all experience points earned.
    - If your logic and reason scores are 16 or higher, you gain a +10% bonus to all experience points earned.
  </FeatureSection>

    <FeatureSection title="Multi-Classing">
        As a Druid, your path is one of strict adherence, and as such you cannot be a multi-class character, even if you meet all other prerequisites. 
    </FeatureSection>

## Druid Features
As a druid, you gain the following class features.

<GameplayCard
  colorSet="fixedfeature"
  title="Druidic"
  subeyebrow="1st Level Druid Feature"
  headerOrder={['title', 'subeyebrow']}
  toc
>
Druids has a secretive language that they all learn as part of their training, allowing them to communicate to other druids without worry of being understood. You can speak, read and write the Druidic language. 
</GameplayCard>

<GameplayCard
  colorSet="System"
  title="The Aspirant's Journey"
  subeyebrow="1st Level Druid Feature"
  headerOrder={['title', 'subeyebrow']}
  toc
>
Druids have a long road ahead of them, and every journey begins as an Aspirant. Upon becoming a Druid, you must devote yourself to one of four philosophical callings: Compassion, Stewardship, Leadership, or Knowledge. Your chosen calling grants you a unique feature and keyword, reflecting the ideals that will guide your journey.

Additionally, the Contemplations granted by your Journey Features possess both passive benefits and Invocations. You can invoke the Invocation benefit of one of your currently selected Contemplations. You can do this once, and regain it's use upon finishing any Night's Rest. 

  <FeatureSection title="Aspirant of Compassion">
    You gain the **Druid of Compassion** keyword.
    
    When you perform the Caregiving downtime activity, characters under your care recover additional grit equal to 1/2 the level of the class that provided this feature.
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Aspirant of Stewardship">
    You gain the **Druid of Stewardship** keyword.
    
   When undertaking Downtime Activities relating to stewardship, farming, animal husbandry, brewing, or crafting, gain a +4 bonus to your chance of success.
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Aspirant of Leadership">
    You gain the **Druid of Leadership** keyword.
    
    Increase the Loyalty bonus granted to your Henchmen by one-half your Druid Level (minimum of 1).
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Aspirant of Knowledge">
    You gain the **Druid of Knowledge** keyword.
    
    You may perform the Research Downtime Activity while undertaking any other Downtime Activity. 
  </FeatureSection>
</GameplayCard>

<GameplayCard
  colorSet="System"
  title="The Worldly Journey"
  subeyebrow="1st Level Druid Feature • Journey Feature"
  headerOrder={['title', 'subeyebrow']}
  toc
>
The Worldly Journey teaches the truths of mortal existence. Through meditation and study, Druids contemplate the archetypes that shape our lives and our place within the world. Select one Contemplation from the list below and gain its benefits. You retain the benefits of your selected Worldly Journey Contemplation until you spend a Downtime Event contemplating another.

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of The Sage">
    While you are Contemplating the Sage, you gain a +1 Magic Bonus to Attribute Tests where your Class Skill Bonus would apply.
    
    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      You can use this invocation after failing any Attribute Test. You can reroll the failed test, but must use the new result.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of the Mystic">
    While you are Contemplating the Mystic, the Detect Magic spell becomes a Druid spell for you. If you end the Contemplation of the Mystic, any spell slots that held the Detect Magic spell are immediately emptied.
    
    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      You can cast the Detect Magic 1st level spell without using a spell slot. When cast this way, the duration is doubled.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of The Mother">
    While you are Contemplating the Mother, when you complete a Night's rest, recover additional Grit equal to one-half your Druid Level (minimum of 1).

    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      You can cast the Cure Wounds 1st level spell without using a spell slot. You can cast the spell this way, even if it is not on your spell list.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of The King">
    While you are Contemplating the King, increase your maximum henchmen by 1.

    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      When a henchmen that can see or hear you fails a Morale check, you can use a Reaction to allow them to reroll the morale check with a +4 bonus.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of The Heirophant">
    While you are Contemplating the Heirophant, any ally who can see an hear you that would not normally be able to participate in an Attribute Test can, but they participate with no Skill Bonus.

    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      When an ally that can see and hear you fails a Attribute Test, you can use a Reaction and allow them to reroll the failed test, but must use the new result.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of The Companion">
    While you are Contemplating the Companion, you can utilize the Help Activity in combat 1 Phase earlier than normal.

    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      You use your Reaction, and allow an ally that can see and hear you to React to something that would normally allow them to React. They can use this feature even if they have acted or Reactied in This Phase, or if they have already used their Reaction.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>

</GameplayCard>

<GameplayCard
  colorSet="System"
  title="Spellcasting"
  subeyebrow="2nd Level Druid Feature"
  headerOrder={['title', 'subeyebrow']}
  toc
>
Through ancient rites and forgotten mysteries, you have learned to prepare miracles within your mind. Each miracle can be held only temporarily. When invoked, it is expended and slips from memory until received again through meditation and ritual.

Your spells are **clerical spells**, and you use the **Free-Hand** casting method. You otherwise follow the general rules in [Chapter 09: Spellcasting](/playersguide/09-spellcasting/).

  <FeatureSection title="Spell Preparation">
  As part of a Safe Rest, you may perform a personal ritual of meditation and communion to prepare your spells.

  You may prepare any spell on the Druid spell list for which you have a spell slot of the appropriate level. The spells you prepare take effect when you complete the Safe Rest. If the Safe Rest is not completed, the spells are not prepared.
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Reversible Spells">
  Each time you prepare a reversible spell, choose whether you prepare its primary or reverse form. The chosen form is treated as the prepared spell.

  Preparing one form does not prepare the other. To have both forms available, you must prepare each separately.
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Casting Spells">
  You use the Free-Hand casting method described in the spellcasting chapter. Your Casting Hand must be free to perform the spell's Components.
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Cantrips">
  Cantrips are simple mysteries that require little effort to prepare.

  When you prepare your spells as part of a Safe Rest, you may also prepare the number of cantrips shown on your Spell Progression table from the Druid cantrip list.

  Your Spell Progression table also shows the number of cantrip casts in your shared pool. You regain all expended cantrip casts when you complete a Night's Rest.
  </FeatureSection>
</GameplayCard>

<GameplayCard
  colorSet="System"
  title="The Inner Journey"
  subeyebrow="5th Level Druid Feature • Journey Feature"
  headerOrder={['title', 'subeyebrow']}
  toc
>
The Inner Journey teaches that wisdom is not freely given, but earned through sacrifice, change, and understanding. Select one Contemplation from the list below and gain its benefits. You retain the benefits of your selected Inner Journey Contemplation until you spend a Downtime Event contemplating another.

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of The Traveller">
    While you are Contemplating the Traveller, increase your base Move Speed by +5 feet.

    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      You can invoke this feature when you perform any movement, and that movement does not provoke Opportunity Actions.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of The Warrior">
    While you are Contemplating the Warrior, you gain a +1 Magic Bonus to Attack Rolls.

    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      You can use this Invocation after missing with an Attack Roll, you can reroll the attack roll, but must use the new result.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of The Guide">
    While you are Contemplating the Guide, you cannot become lost by non-magical mean while travelling.

    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      After invoking this feature, you can ignore the effects of difficult terrain for 1 minute.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of The Turning">
    While you are Contemplating the Turning, if you are suffering any penalties for Attribute Tests, you can select one penalty source and ignore it. This feature does not work on sources that have a -4 penalty or larger.

    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      You can invoke this feature, and spend 1 minute in quiet contemplation, afterwards you can swap any one of your prepared spells with another on your spell list of the appropriate level. If this feature is interuppted, you do not use your invocation.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of The Judge">
    While you are Contemplating the Judge, the Command spell becomes a Druid spell for you. If you end the Contemplation of the Judge, any spell slots that held the Judge spell are immediately emptied. 

    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      You can cast the Command 1st level spell without using a spell slot. When cast using this feature, creatures have a -2 Magic Penalty to their saving throws.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of The Hanged Man">
    While you are Contemplating the Hanged Man, when you make an Attribute Test, you can grant yourself a penalty between -1 and -5 to the test. If the test is successful, the next attribute test you make gains a bonus equal to the penalty you gave yourself +1.

    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      If you are suffering from a temporary effect causing the Blinded, Dazed, Dazzled, or Deafend, you can invoke this feature and end the condition.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of Death">
    While you are Contemplating Death, you may change which cantrips you have prepared whenever you complete a Night's Rest, rather than only after a Safe Rest.
    
    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      When you make a Saving Throw against an effect that would impose a condition but would not kill you outright, you may invoke death and choose to automatically fail. If you do, the condition lasts for only 1 round.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of The Harmonious">
    While you are Contemplating the Harmonious, you can cast the reverse of any spell you have prepared without needing to prepare it separately.

    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      You may use this invocation before making any d20 test. You treat the test as if you rolled a 10.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>
</GameplayCard>

<GameplayCard
  colorSet="System"
  title="The Eternal Journey"
  subeyebrow="10th Level Druid Feature • Journey Feature"
  headerOrder={['title', 'subeyebrow']}
  toc
>
The Eternal Journey concerns itself with the oldest mysteries of existence—the truths that govern life, death, destiny, and enlightenment itself. Select one Contemplation from the list below and gain its benefits. You retain the benefits of your selected Eteral Journey Contemplation until you spend a Downtime Event contemplating another.

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of The Chained">
    While you are Contemplating the Chained, when you make a saving throw against a spell that is on your spell list, you gain a +2 bonus to the saving throw.

    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      As an Action in Phase 1, you can invoke this contemplation. One foe who is adjacent to you cannot willingly move away from you for 1 round.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of The Sundering">
    While you are Contemplating the Sundering, your Druid spells that cause damage deal extra damage equal to one-half your Druid Level. This feature does not affect Cantrips.

    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      You can invoke this contemplation when you make a Saving Throw against an effect that causes damage, and a Successful Saving Throw would allow you to take half damage. If your saving throw is a success, you take no damage. If your saving throw was a failure, you only take half damage.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of The Stargazer">
    While you are Contemplating the Stargazer, you always know the cardinal directions, approximate time, and the direction toward any location you have previously visited while you can see the open sky. Additionally, allies that can see and hear you gain a +1 Magic Bonus to Saving Throws against effects that cause fear or charm.

    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      When you, or an ally that can see and hear you fails a Saving Throw, you can invoke this contemplation to allow them to reroll the Save. This takes no actions to do, and they must accept the new result.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of The Dreamer">
    While you are Contemplating the Dreamer, after finishing a Night's Rest, roll 1d20, and record the result. You can replace any d20 test made by you or a creature that you can see with this roll. If you do not use your roll, you lose it during a Night's Rest, replacing it with a new one when you wake. 

    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      When you invoke this Contemplation, you cast the Augury spell without spending a spell slot, and the spell automatically succeeds.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of The Illuminated">
    While you are Contemplating the Illuminated, if you spend 1 Master Action gazing at a target, you immediately know it's true form. You learn if it being affected by magic or not, and understand it's true nature, even if hidden. Additionally, your GM will inform you of all keywords the target has.

    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      When you invoke this Contemplation, you cast the True Seeing spell upon yourself, without spending a spell slot. You can use this invocation even if the True Seeing spell isn't on your Druid Spell list.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of The Awakened">
    While you are Contemplating the Awakened, when you become Polymorphed by any means, you retain your ability to speak. Additionally, while polymorphed you do not temporarily lose your keywords, and only gain keywords from the polymorph.

    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      When you invoke this Contemplation, you cast the Polymorph spell upon yourself, without spending a spell slot. When cast in this manner, you can choose to triple the duration of the polymorph.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>

  <FeatureSection title="Contemplation of The World">
    While you are Contemplating the World, choose one spell of 5th level or lower from the Cleric spell list and one spell of 5th level or lower from the Magic-User spell list. Both spells are considered Druid spells for you.

    <FeatureSubsection title="Invocation">
      When you invoke this Contemplation, choose and cast one 6th-level spell from any spell list. Afterward, you cannot recover any expended Contemplation Invocations until you complete a Downtime Event.
    </FeatureSubsection>
  </FeatureSection>

</GameplayCard>
