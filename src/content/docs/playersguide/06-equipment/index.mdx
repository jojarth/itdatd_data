# Assumptions

Although a resource tracking game, ITD&TD makes some assumptions about your characters. It is assumed that you have a good backpack. It is assumed that while in towns, you are replacing aging rations, refilling component pouches, performing repairs on gear, keeping weapons in good repair and the like.

# Encumbrance

Heroes must keep well equipped to take on the denizens of the dark dungeons they find themselves in. A good weapon, well maintained armor, and a sturdy shield are just the start. Torches, food, and tools are all needed to ensure survival.

However, you can't just carry everything on your back. Each adventure should be planned, balancing taking everything you need, but no more,  and making calculated risk about what to take and what not to take.

After all, carrying too much could be the difference between life and death, but not carrying enough could be too.

## Resource Management

A big part of the World of Algorion is resource management. Torches, food, oil, water, spell components, extra weapons, all go to make this world a living, breathing place.

Many newer players in the TTRPG scene have expressed disinterest in tracking these items, and as such other TTRPG systems have gotten away from tracking resources. The GM guide will have optional rules if your table prefers these rules.

However, I highly suggest that you attempt using the default rule set, and including the tracking of resources heavily into your game. The game is meant to be an adventuring life simulator, and the threat of going hungry, or the threat of being stuck in a dungeon with no light should influence your decisions.

## Encumbrance Points (EP)

Encumbrance is measured by means of Encumbrance Points, or “EP”. EP is an abstract measure on how much an item weighs, as well as how bulky or hard to carry it is. For example, a short sword and an arming sword are both 1 EP, despite a short sword being obviously easier to carry than an arming sword. Another example is Chain Mail vs a Wooden Shield. Chain Mail has a lower encumbrance rate than that of a wooden shield, despite being heavier. But a wooden shield is much more rigid than chain mail, thus hard to carry and pack. This system is meant to be a little abstract.

## Encumbrance Guidelines

Some encumbrance items have additional needs, for example, armor, coins, and very light items.

### Armor Specific Notes

Armor has 2 different EP measurements, as Armor Impacts you differently if it is worn vs carried. For example, if you are wearing a boiled leather breastplate, it will weight you down a little, and affect your mobility slightly, but if you attempt to shove it in your backpack, the stiff leather simply doesn't lend itself well to being packed away. Although it weighs the same, the stiffness of the leather makes packing around it difficult, taking up a lot of room in your pack, and it’s unwieldy in that case. 

Armor has its EP points listed as "Equipped EP/Packed EP", e.g.; 2/3. A 2/3 armor would count as 2 towards your encumbrance if worn, and 3 towards your encumbrance if packed. 

In order for Armor to use the Equipped EP, it must be donned, and you can only don 1 suit of armor.

### Bundled Items

Some items can be bundled up, items like torches, arrows, crossbow bolts and the like. These items can be bundled together for purposes of determining EP. When an item's EP is listed as "B(#)", it is a bundled item. Owning any number of the item, up to the number listed in parenthesis is equal to 1 EP.

For example, Arrows are a B(20) item. This means that owning anywhere between 1–20 arrows counts as 1 EP, 21–40 arrows counts as 2 EP, and so on.

A GM may decide that some items are simply to inconsequential to track encumbrance on. 

### Like-Items

Some items, such as coins, are technically different items, but should be grouped together for determining EP. For example, when determining EP weight of your coinage, you add the total number of coins you are carried, and use that total for determining the Bundled EP of your coinage. Likewise, you wouldn't track a "Silver Ring" and a "Gold Ring with a gemstone" as 2 different sources of Rings.

### Coinage

Currency (Copper Coins, Silver Coins, and Gold Coins) are a B(100) item. However, they are treated as a Like-Item.

### Additional Guidelines

Since EP is abstract, it's hard to nail down how much constitutes 1 EP. Remember, a bracelet may not take up a lot of room or be heavy, but 50 bracelets do, and could cause issues packing other gear. 

Think not just about the weight of the item, but also the rigidity, bulk, and generally how hard it is to pack not just the item, but other stuff around it. When in doubt, compare an item to something similar listed here.

## Calculating Encumbrance

Tally the total EP of the items you have on your person, then compare them to the Encumbrance Table. It is expected that while on adventures, most adventurers will be moderately to heavily encumbered with the necessary gear. Thus, if you have a Brawn modifier of +1, you become Lightly Encumbered at 5 EP, Moderately Encumbered at 7 and so on. 

| **Encumbrance** | **EP Total** |
| --- | --- |
| **Unencumbered** | 0 |
| **Lightly Encumbered** | 4 + Brawn Mod |
| **Moderately Encumbered** | 7 + Brawn Mod |
| **Heavily Encumbered** | 10 + Brawn Mod |
| **Over Encumbered** | 13 + Brawn Mod |

## Becoming Encumbered

Once your EP is tallied, find out where it lies in your Encumbrance Table. In the above example, we had +1 Brawn Modifier. If we were carrying 5 EP worth of items, that would put us at Lightly Encumbered (As with the +1 Brawn Modifier, it takes 7 EP to cause us to become Moderately Encumbered). Being encumbered causes some small movement penalties.

Higher levels of Encumbrance imparts an Encumbrance Penalty. The penalty listed is applied to your ***Armor Class*** as well as your ***Evasion*** and ***Control Saving Throws.*** Additionally, for each encumbrance penalty, reduce your ***Move Speed*** by 5 feet (to a minimum of 5 feet). 

### Unencumbered

While you are unencumbered, you have no penalties.

### Lightly Encumbered

While you are lightly encumbered, reduce the distance of all jumps by 1 foot.

### Moderately Encumbered

Your Encumbrance penalty while Moderately Encumbered is 1. 

### Heavily Encumbered

Your Encumbrance penalty while Moderately Encumbered is 3. 

### Over Encumbered

Your Encumbrance penalty while Over Encumbered is 6.

## Brawn and Encumbrance

As you can see from the encumbrance table, your brawn modifier is applied to the number of EP you can carry. But what about adventurers with a negative brawn modifier? Low-brawn adventures simply count as encumbered quicker, as you do subtract their brawn mod to determine their EP breakpoints. For example, if your brawn modifier was -2, your Lightly encumbered total becomes 2. Thus, they can only carry 2 EP worth of item before becoming Lightly encumbered.

Essentially, low brawn characters are very easy to burden, and the encumbrance system is used to reflect this.

## Size and Encumbrance

The Encumbrance totals listed are for small and medium sized creatures. Creatures smaller than small, and creatures larger than medium gain a bonus or penalty to their maximum EP they can carry. Often times, larger creatures also have a higher brawn score, leading to large creatures being able to carry quite a bit more.

However, it should be noted that EP points are calculated in this initial guide for creatures of medium and small sizes. The EP cost of larger and smaller gear isn't calculated here.

To determine your new breakpoints based on size, simply add the EP Adjustment to all values above. Thus, a Huge creature can carry 8 + brawn Mod worth of EP before becoming Lightly Encumbered, and Huge creatures will likely have a decent brawn score as well.

| **Size** | **EP Adjustment** |
| --- | --- |
| **Tiny** | -2 |
| **Small** | 0 |
| **Medium** | 0 |
| **Large** | +2 |
| **Huge** | +4 |
| **Gargantuan** | +8 |
| **Colossal** | +16 |

# Wielding Items

Sometimes a rule asks that you are "Wielding" a weapon or object. Wielding an object means preparing to use the object as it was intended. For example, to “wield” a sword, you must have it in your hand so you can swing it around. 

With a few exceptions, you are allowed to wield up to two items, one in each hand, and many interactions require that you are wielding an item to use it. For example, you can only attack with a wielded weapon, or benefit from a shield if it is wielded. 

## Free Hand

Many interactions require you have a free hand. A free hand is one that is not wielding anything (and indeed, not holding anything either). Most spell-casting requires a free hand, or that you wield specific items in one of your hands.

## Weapons

You can only wield one weapon at a time. If you wield a second weapon, you must announce which one you are wielding, and until you spend a ***Master Action*** to shift your focus, the other weapon you are wielding is simply dead weight. 

### Two-Weapon Fighting

The exception to the above rule is if both weapons have the Light weapon property. While both weapons you are wielding contain the Light weapon properties (and are of bundled EP if you are a small character), you can wield both weapons, and select which one to attack with when you make the appropriate action to attack with a weapon.

## Aspergillum

A small tool use to dispense or sprinkle Holy Water. An Aspergillum comes in 2 forms. both with the same Cost and EP.

### Holy Branch

A specially prepared branch of a tree considered to be holy to an order, then specially prepared and blessed. To spread Holy Water from a Holy Branch, you must pair your Aspergillum with an Aspersorium, dipping the branch in the liquid contained in the Aspersorium and flicking it towards your target.

As an *Action* in the *Volley Phase (1)*, you can dip your Holy Branch in an Aspersorium full of liquid, and it will hold that liquid for 5 Rounds. 

As a *Master Action* on the *Miscellaneous Phase (4)*, you can whip the branch, attempting to sprinkle the liquid you dipped it in on a target within 5 feet of you. When you do so, you reduce the number of rounds that the Holy Branch can hold the liquid by 1 (this will likely mean that it will be reduced by 2 rounds total this round, one for using it, and one at the end of the round). 

Due to the amount of liquid a holy branch holds, your main target automatically gets covered in the oil. Additionally, all foes adjacent to your target may get hit by this as well. Make a Dexterity Attack Roll using your proficiency in the Flails Weapon Group, which sets the DC for your secondary targets. Your Secondary Targets must make ***Reflex Saving Throws.*** If they fail, they are coated by the liquid, and they are not if they succeed. 

### Mace

Although not technically a weapon, the Mace Aspergillum looks very similar to a Light Mace. It is lighter, hollow, and contains a reservoir with a sponge soaked in liquid. Treat this liquid as if it had 10 charges. As a *Master Action* on the *Miscellaneous Phase (4)* you can whip the mace, attempting to sprinkle the liquid located inside on a target within 5 feet of you. When you do so, reduce the charges of this mace by 1. Additionally, the mace looses 3 charges every 8 hours. 

You can refill the mace back up to 10 charges by using a *Master Action* on the *Volley Phase (1)* to dip the mace into an Aspersorium and swirl it around, or by opening it up and pouring a flask of liquid into the reservoir, allowing the sponge to soak the liquid. If different liquids are in the mace, none function, and you must spend 1 minute ringing the sponge out and rinsing it to get the mace to function properly.

When flicking the mace, you make an Attack Roll using Dexterity and your Proficiency in the Maces weapon group. The result of this attack roll sets the DC. Your target then makes a Reflex Saving Throw. If they fail, they are coated in the liquid. If they succeed, they dodge the liquid. 

Finally, although not a weapon, you can use the Mace as a weapon. When wielded so, you treat it like a Light Mace that cannot use any of the normal mace Proficiency functions. Each time you attack, you use 1 Charge, hit or miss. However, if you hit a target, they are automatically coated in the Liquid, no saving throw. When you hit a creature, roll a 1d6, on a result of 1, your Aspergillum breaks, and becomes useless. 

## Aspersorium

An aspersorium is a holy tool that holds liquid for an Aspergillum. Aspergillums are dipped into the aspersorium to absorb the liquid located inside. It should be noted that aspersoria are very difficult to use during combat. Although a filled Aspersorium is 2 EP, it is still wielded with 1 hand. It is effectively an open bucket, pot, or vase that you must carry around to dip your aspergillum in to use it effectively. 

An empty Aspersorium is 1 EP. A filled Aspersorium is 2 EP due to the added liquid and the care required to keep the open vessel upright. Due to how they spread the liquid, they can potentially be much better at extending how much liquid you use. You can place a flask of liquid into an Aspersorium, granting it 3 Charges. An Aspersorium can hold up to 9 charges of liquid (3 flasks worth). 

Dipping an aspergillum into an aspersorium will recharge the aspergillum (see above), by using 1 charge of the aspersorium. 

However, while you have an aspersorium with charges on your person, each time you are attacked, or must make a ***Reflex Saving Throw***, you reduce the charges by 1.

## Backpack

It is assumed that you have a backpack. If you do not, your GM will put limits on how much you can carry. Every character gains a backpack upon character creation, and the list here is simply for the cost of a replacement. 

## Crowbar

If you carry a crowbar, you gain a +1 Situation to Strength Skill Tests where the leverage of the crowbar can be used.

## Flask of Oil (or Phial)

A flask of oil can be used to refill lanterns, or help further the effects of fire. It can be poured directly from the flask, or placed in an aspergillum. 

A flask of oil can be in a glass phial, which can be thrown as a Splash Weapon with your Sling Proficiency. Throwing it this way creates a 5-foot burst, using the rules of Splash Weapons. 

Creatures caught in the burst gain Vulnerable 5 (Fire), a -2 Situation to saving throws made to avoid effects that deal fire damage, and have an increased chance of being caught aflame. This lasts for 1d6 rounds, although a creature can use a *Master Action* to wipe off the oil on themselves, or an adjacent creature, ending the effect early. 

## Garlic

A common spell component, as well as an effective deterrent for many creatures. 

## Grappling Hook

Can be attached to rope to attempt to latch a rope onto high-ground to climb.

## Small Hammer (or Mallet)

Not large enough to be used as a weapon, nor manufactured for that reason, a small hammer or mallet allows you to drive metal or wooden stakes into objects, or gives you a +1 Situation to Strength Skill Tests where the crushing force of a hammer-like tool can be used. If you attempt to use this as a weapon, this tool would belong to the bludgeons group, have the ***Light*** weapon property, deal d4 damage, and have no features for your Proficiency with the Bludgeon Weapon Group.

## Holy Water

Holy Water is used in many rituals, and as a spell component for many spells. Additionally, many undead creatures react poorly to being covered in Holy Water. A phial of holy water can can be poured directly from the phial, or placed in an aspergillum. 

A phial of holy water can be thrown as a Splash Weapon with your Sling Proficiency. Throwing it this way creates a 5-foot burst, using the rules of Splash Weapons. 

Undead creatures in the splash zone of the Holy Water will take Sacred damage based on the Spell Used to create the Holy Water, as well as the Spell Bonus of the Caster who created it. Below are a few examples of Holy Waters, and their costs. Note that Blessed Water can be somewhat common in limited supply, but any other water should be very limited, and likely to be player-created, and exceedingly rare.   

| **Holy Water** | **Damage** | **Cost** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blessed Water | 2d6 Sacred Damage | 25 Silver |
| Sanctified Water | 4d6 Sacred Damage | 1 Gold |
| Consecrated Water | 6d6 Sacred Damage | 5 Gold |
| Hallowed Water | 8d6 Sacred Damage | 10 Gold |

Undead creatures with Turning Resistance reduces the damage dice by 1 step. Undead creatures with Turning Protection also reduces the damage dice by 1 step. If an undead creature has both, they do not take damage from Holy Water. 

## Iron Spike

Iron Spikes can be used to drive into wood and stone to hold doors open or closed, or they can be fashioned and made into pitons for climbing safely. 

## Lantern

Lanterns provide the same light as a torch, but are harder to put out. However, they are heavier, and require oil to keep going. 1 Flask of Oil is enough for 1 hour of light.

## Manacles

Manacles can be used to bind the hands or feet of another creature. There are several quality of manacles.

### Low Quality

The DC to escape these manacles is 17. The DC to pick the lock on them is 15. The DC to break them with your Strength is 25. These manacles are made of low-quality iron.

### Moderate Quality

The DC to escape these manacles is 20. The DC to pick the lock on them is 18. The DC to break them with your Strength is 28. These manacles are made of low-quality iron.

### High Quality

The DC to escape these manacles is 23. The DC to pick the lock on them is 21. The DC to break them with your Strength is 31. These manacles are made of low-quality iron.

### Superior Quality

The DC to escape these manacles is 26. The DC to pick the lock on them is 24. The DC to break them with your Strength is 34. These manacles are made of low-quality iron.

## Mirror

In addition to being the Spell Component for many spells, mirrors are great for a variety of reasons, including looking around corners.

## Rations

Both types of rations provide 1 day's worth of food. Many Exploration Events can affect your Trail Rations but will not affect Iron Rations. Trail Rations are more pleasant to eat, but they are less durable and more difficult to pack efficiently for a long journey.

### Trail Rations

However, Trail Rations are only good for about 1 week, and spoil easy, leading to many Exploration Events causing them to be unable to provide nutrition. 

However, trail rations are also much more tasty than Iron Rations, and increase your comfort level. 

### Iron Rations

Iron Rations remain edible practically indefinitely and are packed densely for travel. However, they simply do not provide the same level of taste or comfort as Trail Rations.

## Rope

A 50 foot length of braided hemp rope, or other fibrous plants. 

## Spell Books

Spell books are required for practitioners of Goetic magic and Thaumaturgy to prepare spells from. Newly purchased spell books contain no spells, but are created out of premium and durable materials and enchanted, which is why the price is so high. 

### Spell Book (Standard)

These books must be built study, using leather-wrapped wood for the covers, thick, specially treated leather for the spine, and a thick, premium vellum for the pages. They are quite large as well, measuring between 16 - 18 inches wide, 12 - 15 inches tall, and 2-3 inches thick. Standard Spell books can hold 30 levels of spells within them.

### Spell Book (Travel)

Similar in construction, although not size, travel spell books allow a user to prioritize weight over spell count. Travel Spell Books can only hold 10 levels worth of spells in them. 

## Spell Component Pouch

By having a spell component pouch on your character, you are assumed to have all *Level 1 material components* for any and all spells. 

## Stake (Wooden)

A wooden stake is great for miscellaneous tasks, but are also great for staking vampires through the heart. 

## **Thieves’ Tools**

A thief must have a set of thieves tools on them in order to ***Disarm Fine Traps*** or ***Open Locks***.

## **Tinder Box**

Contains all you need to start non-magical fires with relative ease, a feat otherwise difficult, but doable without them. 

## **Torches**

Provides much needed light. A torch burns for 1 hour, although some effects may cause it to go out quicker. Torches provide bright light in a 20 foot radius, and dim light 20 feet out after that. 

## **Waterskin\Wineskin**

A water- or wine-holding receptacle made from animal skin and treated with solutions of bark or other plants. A full skin is 1 EP. Up to four empty skins can be bundled together for 1 EP.

## **Wood Pole (10 ft)**

A useful pole used for long-distance poking. Hard to carry, but triggering a trap from 10 feet away can be the difference of life or death.

# Holy Symbols

| **Item** | **Cost** | **EP** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Amulet | 15 Silver | 0 worn / 1 packed |
| Emblem | 15 Silver | 0 worn / 1 packed |
| Phylactery | 20 Silver | 0 worn / 1 packed |
| Reliquary | 5 Silver | 2 |
| Symbol | - | 0 worn / 1 packed |
| Tefillah | 20 Silver | 0 worn / 1 packed |

Only one properly worn holy symbol can have an EP of 0. Additional holy symbols use their packed EP.

## Amulet

A holy symbol presented as an amulet is worn on a length of chain or cord attached to your neck. You must have a free hand to hold the amulet, or grasp the amulet when used as a spell focus.

## Emblem

A holy symbol presented as an emblem is a carving of your holy symbol in a holy material, such as special woods, stones, or metals. Often it was worn on a chain, attached to your belt. You must have a free hand to hold or grasp the emblem when used as a spell focus. 

## Phylactery or Tefillah

A holy symbol presented as a phylactery, or tefillah, is a pair of small, specially prepared boxes made of specially prepared leather. Inside of the box is a small number of scriptures that the individual wearer finds particularly poignant for the way they practice their faith. One box is strapped to your arm, and one to your head. Although you can hold other objects with the arm that has the phylactery or tefillah, in order to use it as a holy symbol, that hand must be free, and you must touch it to the phylactery or tefillah on your head. 

## Reliquary

A reliquary is a container for other holy texts or symbols. It is often not used by adventurers, but rather non-adventuring, and non-traveling priests. Despite being a 2 EP object, you only need to place a hand on a reliquary to use it as a holy symbol. 

## Symbol

A symbol is just a token of your faith that may or may not be in-line with standard practices. For example, a priest who worships nature may use specially prepared plants that a druid might use. A generic symbol should generally cost 5 silver. It is 0 EP while properly worn as your primary holy symbol and 1 EP while packed. 

You must have one free hand to interact with any symbol when used as a Holy Symbol.

## Common Saddle

While riding a mount in a common saddle, any time you are being forced to dismount, you have a 10% chance to stay in the saddle instead. See Mounted Combat for additional details. 

## Military Saddle

While riding a mount in a military saddle, any time you are forced to dismount, you have a 50% chance to stay in the saddle instead. However, you have a -3 Situation on any d20 Test made to Swiftly Dismount your saddle. 

## Knight’s Saddle

While riding a mount in a military saddle, any time you are forced to dismount, you have a 75% chance to stay in the saddle instead. However, you cannot Swiftly Dismount from your steed, and must instead perform a normal dismount.

# Shields

Shields provide multiple layers of protection, and many adventurers won’t leave home without one. In addition to passive bonuses just for wielding one, shields can be used to reduce damage as well. Please note that you can only benefit from wielding 1 shield at a time.

## Critical Protection

If you are proficient with a shield you are wielding, you automatically gain ***Critical Protection.*** Critical Protection causes foes who roll a natural 20 on their dice roll to require a critical confirmation roll to critically hit you. See the combat section for more details.

## Reading the Shield Table

Shields have the following properties.

### Type

This is the type of shield you are using. For example, you could be using a Small Wood shield. The type (Buckler, Small, Medium and Body) usage is determined by your Proficiency with the shield, but the material (Wood, Reinforced or Metal) is not.

Wood is cheap and light, reinforced is a little more expensive, but has some additional properties that make it a good balance between wood and metal shields. Metal shields are very heavy and expensive, but offer greater protection.

### Cost

This is simply the cost of the item in question. Your GM may alter this cost based on availability, but this cost serves as the “inexpensive” shield. For example, it would be difficult to find a metal body shield (or even a body shield at all) in the far north reaches of Aslund, and a GM may double or triple the price of one, or simply say they aren't available.

Cost is listed in Copper (C), Silver (S), or Gold (G).

### EP (Equipped/Packed)

This is how many Encumbrance Points your shield is worth. The equipped value applies while your shield is wielded and ready to be used. The packed value applies while the shield is carried but not wielded. Shields are large and awkward, making them more difficult to pack than to wear ready for use.

### AC Bonus

When you wield a shield, you apply this bonus to your Armor Class. If you are proficient with the shield you are using, you apply this bonus to your Reflex Saving Throws from sources you can see as well.

### Block Value

When you perform a Shield Block, this is the amount of damage you reduce the attack by.

### Break Dice

When you perform a Shield Block, this is the dice you roll to see if your shield's condition worsens. 

### Break Chance

When you perform a Shield Block, you roll your break dice, and if the result of the dice is your Break Chance or lower, your shield’s condition worsens. 

There are a lot of functions that can affect Break Chance, but the most common are the Breaching and Denting Weapon Features. If a function calls for you to treat the break chance higher, raise the number you need to roll. 

If your Break Chance would be lowered to 0 or lower, you still have a chance to break your shield. You must roll your Break Die twice, and if both results are a 1, your shield’s condition still worsens. 

### Check Penalty

While you are wielding the shield, it grants you a situation penalty to Strength and Dexterity Skill Tests. Note that if you have a Check Penalty from both your Armor and a shield, you only apply the worse of the two penalties, rather than stacking them up.

Shield check penalties may not apply to certain Skill Test, for example, you do not apply a Shield's check penalty to Skill Tests made to avoid being knocked prone or restrained in combat, as in those circumstances you can leverage your shield in a positive manner. Shield check penalties mostly apply to out-of-combat Skill Tests. Check with your GM before applying a Shield's Check Penalty over your Armor's Check penalty.

# Shield Block

The final, and perhaps most important, benefit of using a shield is the ability to use your Reaction to Shield Block. You must be proficient with the shield you are wielding to perform a shield block.

A shield block allows your shield to absorb some damage, at the cost of potentially breaking. Shield Blocks are gone over in the combat section. 

# Shield Conditions

Like other equipment, shields are subject to equipment conditions. From best to worst, these conditions are Good > Damaged > Broken > Destroyed.

## Condition List

As your shield gets damaged, it’s performance degrades.

### Good

A shield in the good condition uses the normal shield statistics. 

### Damaged

A shield that is damage is usable, but flawed. The AC bonus and block value are unaffected, but the Break Chance is increased by 1 point. A damaged shield is very much usable, but will become worse much easier.

### Broken

A broken shield starts to loose it's defensive properties, although it can still be used. Reduce the AC a broken shield provides by 1 point, and reduce the amount of damage you can block with a shield block by 1. Finally, the break chance of a broken shield is increased by 1 point.

### Destroyed

A destroyed shield is no longer usable, and can no longer be repaired by mundane means.

## Repairing a Shield

A damaged or broken shield (but not a destroyed one) can be repaired if you spend time to do so. It should be noted that you can never repair a shield past 1 point higher than its lowest point. Thus, if you have a Broken Shield, you can repair it to damaged, but that shield will never be in good repair again.

Repairing a shield can be done as a downtime event, or even as part of field repairs.

# Shield Materials

The most common shields are Wooden, or wood reinforced with metal, or just metal. However, some races may use other materials for a shield, such as bone. The GM may alter a shield's properties for such materials. For example, a bone shield may have its Break Dice increased by 3 points over a Wood Shield counterpart, leading to quickly breaking bone shields.

The GMs guide will have additional details on shield materials.

# Weapon and Armor Kits

Although not as popular as the equipment kits above, you can use some of these kits to quickly purchase weapons and armor. Like the equipment kits above, these are simply a fast way to select some common “load-outs” for adventures who started their career in a particular area. 

These kits assume you are wearing any armor and wielding any shield granted to you in the Encumbrance Calculations.

## Warrior of Aslund

Many warriors who come from Aslund choose to wear medium armor, usually made of a light mail shirt to cover their vitals, with thick or layered animal skins on non-vial areas. Aslund can get very cold in the winter, and their copious use of animal hides reflect this. 

Most warriors will use inexpensive axes, since they double as tools. More wealthy warriors will pick up a sword, and these swords often serve a generation or two before being retired. 

Although the warriors of Aslund are depicted in other cultures as hulking beasts wearing no armor and wielding gigantic weapons in two hands, they are more practical warriors, utilizing heavy armor and shields, along with one-handed weapons.

### Militia Warrior Kit

This kit costs 72 silver, and weighs 6 EP. It contains a battle axe, dagger, suit of medium armor, and a medium wooden shield. 

### Wealthy Warrior Kit

This kit costs 91 silver, and weighs 6 EP. It contains a war sword, a hatchet, a suit of medium armor, and a medium wooden shield.

## Warriors of Oraktos

Many warriors of Oraktos choose to wear little to no armor. Oraktos has hot, dry summers, and mild, wet winters, and because of this, it becomes difficult to keep moisture out of armor, and thus many warriors fight off lice, dirt, fleas, and disease by not caring for their armor. 

However, most warriors from Oraktos choose to use a shield and spear. Most warriors in the region are trained with spears from a young age.

### Wealthy Warrior Kit

This kit costs 66 silver and 10 copper, and weighs 8 EP. It contains a long spear, 2 javelins, a suit of medium armor, and a medium wooden shield.
