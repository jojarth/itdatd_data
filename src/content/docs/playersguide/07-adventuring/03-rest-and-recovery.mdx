## Night's Rest

A **Night's Rest** is the rest an adventurer needs to keep going: sleep, food, and enough quiet to let body and nerve recover. Despite its name, it need not happen at night. A party that delves through the dark hours and sleeps through the day still takes a Night's Rest.

### Completing a Night's Rest

A Night's Rest takes **10 hours**. This time covers everything a party does to make and break camp: setting up, eating, resting, standing watch, preparing for the day ahead, passing the time, and tearing down.

You can benefit from a Night's Rest or a Safe Rest only once every **14 hours**, counted from the end of your last rest of either kind. A day of adventuring followed by a Night's Rest therefore makes a full 24-hour day.

**Interruptions.** A Night's Rest can survive a brief disturbance, such as a short fight or a scramble to put out a fire. If the interruptions total **1 hour or less**, the rest still completes, but counts as interrupted (see Reduced Recovery). If they total more than 1 hour, the Night's Rest fails and grants none of its benefits.

### Benefits

When you complete a Night's Rest, you gain the following benefits:

- You regain all expended cantrip casts.
- You regain the use of any feature that recovers on a Night's Rest.
- You recover Grit equal to **one quarter of your maximum Grit**, rounded down.

### Reduced Recovery

Each of the following is a **rest penalty**:

- You suffer a [condition](/playersguide/07-adventuring/05-afflictions-and-conditions/) marked **Impairs Rest** for at least half of the rest.
- You did not eat enough food or drink enough water that day (see Provisions and Survival).
- Your rest was interrupted, but still completed.

<div class="green-table compact-table">

| Rest Penalties | Grit Recovered |
|:--|:--|
| None | One quarter of your maximum Grit |
| 1 | One fifth of your maximum Grit |
| 2 | One tenth of your maximum Grit |
| 3 | 1 Grit |

</div>

Always round down. You always recover at least 1 Grit from a completed Night's Rest.

:::note[Example]{icon="information"}
A Fighter with a maximum Grit of 43 recovers 10 Grit from a full Night's Rest. If she went to sleep Exhausted, she recovers 8 instead. If she was also short on water that day, she recovers only 4.
:::

## Safe Rest

A **Safe Rest** is the deep, untroubled rest that only true comfort, safety, and shelter can provide. Adventurers most often find it at an inn, but any place that truly offers all three will serve.

### Requirements

A Safe Rest takes **10 hours**, the same as a Night's Rest. In addition, you must have:

- **Comfort, safety, and shelter.** The place itself must provide all three. Your own precautions are not enough.
- **Food and drink** enough for the day.

A place counts only if it truly offers all three. **Comfort** assumes reasonable lodgings: a bed, or at least a good bedroll on a clean, dry floor. Sleeping in a friendly host's barracks with a good bedroll counts, and so does the village baker's floor when the inn is full. Bedding down in the dirt of a stable does not, even within the town walls. Neither does barring a dungeon door and sleeping behind it, or a camp in the wilderness, however well hidden or guarded.

### Benefits

When you complete a Safe Rest, you gain all the benefits of a Night's Rest. You also regain the use of anything that recovers on a Safe Rest, including preparing your spells (see [Chapter 09: Spellcasting](/playersguide/09-spellcasting/)).

### Falling Short of a Safe Rest

A Safe Rest allows no rest penalties. If you would suffer any rest penalty, such as a condition that impairs rest, a missed meal, or an interruption, your rest is a Night's Rest instead, with its normal penalties.

This means lasting conditions are especially costly. A spellcaster who is Exhausted can sleep in the finest inn in the city and still wake without fresh spells.

:::note[Example]{icon="information"}
A Magic-User with a maximum Grit of 20 spends the night at an inn after a hard delve, but she is Exhausted. Her rest counts as a Night's Rest with one rest penalty. She recovers 4 Grit and regains her cantrip casts, but cannot prepare her spells.
:::

## Recovering Hit Points

Hit Points represent real wounds to the body: cuts, gashes, punctures, and deep bruising. Unlike Grit, they heal slowly, and only with true rest.

### Healing Track

Keep a **Healing Track** on your character sheet. Each time you complete a rest without having taken any Hit Point damage since your last rest, add marks to it based on your **Healing Rate**, which is set by your Heartiness (see [Attributes](/playersguide/02-character_creation/01-attributes/)):

- **Safe Rest:** marks equal to your Healing Rate.
- **Night's Rest:** marks equal to half your Healing Rate, rounded down.

<div class="green-table compact-table">

| Healing Rate | Safe Rest | Night's Rest |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 1 | 1 mark | 0 marks |
| 2 | 2 marks | 1 mark |
| 3 | 3 marks | 1 mark |
| 4 | 4 marks | 2 marks |
| 5 | 5 marks | 2 marks |

</div>

Rest penalties do not affect your Healing Track; only Hit Point damage does. When your Healing Track reaches **10 marks**, you recover 1 Hit Point and clear it. If you take any Hit Point damage, clear your Healing Track.

## Lasting Injuries

Some wounds are worse than lost Hit Points. Broken bones, torn muscle, and similar harm are **lasting injuries**, covered in full in [Lasting Injuries](/playersguide/07-adventuring/lasting-injuries/).
