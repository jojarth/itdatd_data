The most dangerous threats in the world are not always blades. Poisons, diseases, and curses work their way into a body over minutes, days, or years, and the states they leave behind can be as deadly as any wound.

## Afflictions vs. Conditions

An **affliction** is something that harms you over time: a poison in your blood, a disease running its course, or a curse laid upon you. A **condition** is a state of being, such as being Paralyzed or Exhausted.

Afflictions often cause conditions, the way a fever leaves you Exhausted. Conditions never cause afflictions.

## Conditions

### General Rules

A **condition** changes your state of being. An effect that gives you a condition tells you how long it lasts. When it ends, you stop suffering (or benefiting from) everything it does.

**Positive and Negative Conditions.** Every condition is either **positive**, such as a Battle Trance, or **negative**, such as being Dazed. Positive conditions help you, even if they carry some drawbacks; negative conditions harm you.

**Effects.** A condition card lists everything the condition does, each effect under its own title. When a condition ends, all of its effects end with it. A few effects, such as Action Delay or Hindered, appear on many cards and in spells; their full rules are under *Combat Effects* in [Chapter 08: Combat](/playersguide/08-combat/#combat-effects), and each card's title links there.

**Condition Modifiers.** Bonuses and penalties described on a condition card are Condition modifiers unless the card says otherwise. Only the greatest Condition penalty and the highest Condition bonus apply to a roll, but they stack with modifiers of other types (see *Static Bonus Types* in [Chapter 01: Core Rules](/playersguide/01-core_rules/#static-bonus-types-d20-rolls)).

**Multiple Conditions.** Each condition states how multiple instances of it combine: use only the highest value, add the values together, or allow only one at a time.

**Ending Conditions.** An effect that ends conditions always names the conditions it ends. There is no general effect that removes "a condition."

**Conditions and Rest.** Some conditions impair rest. If a condition marked **Impairs Rest** lasts at least half of a rest, it imposes a rest penalty (see [Rest and Recovery](/playersguide/07-adventuring/03-rest-and-recovery/)).

## Afflictions

Afflictions come in three types: **poisons**, **diseases**, and **curses**. Each is described by an affliction card.

### Reading an Affliction

* **Type.** Poison, disease, or curse. The type decides how the affliction progresses (see Stages).
* **Save.** The Saving Throw you make against the affliction, usually Save vs Doom for poisons and diseases and Save vs Spell for curses. A potent affliction lists a penalty, which applies only to the save you make when you are first exposed.
* **Virulent.** A Virulent affliction is stubborn. Each time you make a save against one of its stages, roll twice; you succeed only if both rolls succeed.
* **Exposure.** How the affliction is caught, such as through an injury, by being ingested or inhaled, by contact, by being near someone already afflicted, or by magic.
* **Onset.** How long after a failed exposure save the affliction takes hold. During the onset, you suffer no effects.
* **Maximum Duration.** Poisons and diseases end once this much time has passed, no matter which stage you are at. Curses have no maximum duration.
* **Remedy.** How a curse can be lifted (see Curses).

### Exposure

When you are exposed to an affliction, make the listed Saving Throw. On a success, you are unaffected. On a failure, you are afflicted: once the onset passes, you enter Stage 1.

### Stages

Each affliction is divided into stages. Each stage lists its effects and an **interval**. You suffer a stage's effects for as long as you are at that stage. When you move to a different stage, you lose the old stage's effects and gain the new one's.

At the end of each interval, you make another Saving Throw against the affliction:

* **Poisons and diseases.** On a success, move down one stage. On a failure, move up one stage. If you reach Stage 0, the affliction ends. If you fail while already at the highest stage, you remain there and suffer its effects again.
* **Curses.** On a failure, the curse advances one stage. On a success, it holds where it is. A curse never moves down on its own and never ends on its own; it must be lifted (see Curses).

**Damage.** Damage listed in a stage is dealt when you enter that stage, and again each time a failed save keeps you there. Like all damage, it reduces your Grit first unless the stage says it deals **Hit Point damage**. Hit Point damage from an affliction never offers the choice of a Lasting Injury.

### Repeated Exposure

You can suffer several different afflictions at once; track each separately. If two afflictions impose the same condition or effect, follow its normal stacking rules.

Being exposed to a disease or curse you already have does nothing more. Poisons are different: each additional exposure calls for a new save, and each failure moves you up one stage, even during the onset. The onset does not restart. For example, if a second poisoned arrow strikes you during a poison's onset and you fail your save, you enter Stage 2 instead of Stage 1 when the onset ends.

### Afflictions in Combat

* **Exposure.** Make your exposure save in the same phase you are exposed. If the affliction has no onset, its Stage 1 effects begin at the end of that phase.
* **Stage saves.** All stage saves made during combat happen at the end of the round, even if the stage's interval is shorter or began partway through the round. A new stage's effects apply from the next round.

### Curses

Curses are slow, patient afflictions, with stages measured in months or years. Your saves can hold a curse back, but never drive it out. A curse ends only through its **remedy**: usually magic that lifts curses, though a curse may also name a remedy of its own, such as atoning at a shrine or destroying the object that binds it. Magic that lifts curses may only reduce a curse's stage; if a curse is reduced to Stage 0, it ends. A curse that is only partly lifted continues to advance at its next interval.

### Lasting Effects

Some afflictions leave marks that outlast them, most often Attribute Drain (see [Chapter 01: Core Rules](/playersguide/01-core_rules/#attribute-drain)). Lasting effects are listed on the affliction's card and are dealt with separately from the affliction itself.

### Example Affliction

<GameplayCard

  title="Ghoul's Touch Example"
>
A thief's dagger, coated in Ghoul's Touch, strikes your fighter in Phase 5. You make a Save vs Doom with a -2 penalty and fail. The poison has no onset, so at the end of Phase 5 your fighter is at Stage 1: acting a phase late, and stiff in tests of Agility and Precision.

At the end of the round, you save again and fail, moving to Stage 2. Next round, your fighter acts 2 phases late and suffers a -2 penalty to attack rolls. At the end of that round you fail again: Stage 3. Now your fighter has only 2 Actions, each attack costs 2 of them, and every foe holds Combat Supremacy against them.

One more failure brings Stage 4, and your fighter is Paralyzed. Stage 4's interval is 10 minutes, so you now save every 10 minutes instead of every round. Once you succeed, you drop to Stage 3 and are no longer Paralyzed, though you are still badly stiffened and back to saving at the end of each round. When the poison finally ends, by reaching Stage 0 or after 1 hour, your fighter suffers 1 point of Agility drain for having reached Stage 4.
</GameplayCard>
