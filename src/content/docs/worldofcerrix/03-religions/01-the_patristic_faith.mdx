## The Patristic Faith

The Patristic Faith teaches that all creation proceeds from a single God, often called "the Father," the First Begetter, who ordered the world through will, word, and lawful generation. Mortals are not accidents of nature but descendants of an intentional lineage, bound to the Father by obligation, inheritance, and debt. To know one's place in the world is to acknowledge the Father's authority and the order He imposed upon all things.

### Doctrine and the Church

The Patristic tradition holds that the Father revealed His will through appointed forebears (prophets, patriarchs, and witnesses) whose words form the sacred body of law and doctrine. These teachings are preserved, interpreted, and enforced by the Church, which claims unbroken succession from those first speakers of truth. Orthodoxy is not merely correct belief but faithful transmission: to alter the doctrine is to break the chain of descent and invite chaos.

### Sin and Redemption

Sin, in Patristic thought, is rebellion against rightful authority: the child defying the Father, the servant denying the master, the creature refusing its place in the order of being. Redemption comes through obedience, repentance, and restoration to the proper hierarchy, often sealed by sacrifice and solemn vow. Thus the faith is rich in ritual, oath-binding, and judgment, and its clergy act as both spiritual fathers and stern magistrates.

### Many Churches, One Father

There are many different ways to worship the Father, and so there are many different Patristic religions. They all share roughly the same worship but differ in the details, and those differences have led to holy wars in His name. They have also led most Patristic religions to form military chapters and armed orders. This is why clerics, templars, and theurges are so common in Cerrix, and why so many of them become adventurers.

Over the last few hundred years, the Patristic Faith has spread across Enolin, and other religions are becoming less and less common.
